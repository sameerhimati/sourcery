The ranking, and the part rankings hide

Each provider returned eight pages per question. A judge saw all eight and answered one question: could someone answer from these alone? That is the score below, on a scale where 0 is nothing useful and 3 is a full answer.

Three of these providers cannot be told apart. Their intervals overlap, which means the ordering between them is noise — run it again next week and they may swap. That is drawn rather than footnoted, because it is the single most useful thing on the page.

What to actually use

Best overall
Perplexity

Clear of the field, and the cheapest tier of all. Returns a page that fully answers the question about half the time — roughly 1.4× anyone else.

Best value
Serper

Matches Tavily's score at a fifty-fourth of the price. It returns links and snippets, never page content — so pair it with your own fetch and skip paying for bundled extraction.

Hardest to fool
Firecrawl

On the 12 questions with no answer anywhere, it produced the fewest convincing near-misses — beating Perplexity on all 12 of them. For an agent, nothing downstream catches that mistake.

Not what you think it is
Brave

Scores well, but it is not returning pages. Its content is search snippets joined together, with the entities undecoded. Fine for grounding a summary, no use to an agent that must read a document.

Easy questions make everyone look alike

Half the set was written to be hard: multi-hop, ambiguous, or needing a source that is not the first result. The leaders barely notice. The bottom falls out.

Price buys nothing

The same 204 questions cost between twenty cents and seventeen dollars depending on who you asked. The three cheapest providers take three of the top four places.

“Page text” means something different to every one of them

None of this shows up in a relevance score, and it decides what you can build. A provider that strips every link cannot support a crawler. One that returns no tables cannot answer from a spec sheet.

What this cannot see

Every source was cut to 1,600 characters

That cut bound on 98% of Firecrawl's pages and 40% of Perplexity's. A provider returning a 40KB article and one returning 1,600 characters of stitched snippets reached the judge the same size. So this measures which pages you get back, and is blind to how deeply a provider extracts them. For Firecrawl that is the result rather than a caveat beside it — extraction is what it sells, and this run does not test it.

Providers overlap, and a page is rated once

The same page often comes back from several providers. It is judged once and the rating counts for everyone who returned it, using the longest version anyone returned. 66.8% of the 7,496 unique pages came from exactly one provider, and only 24 came from all eight. These indexes overlap far less than you would guess.

Nobody abstains

All eight returned a full set of results on the questions that have no answer, exactly as they do everywhere else. Zero empty results across the run. Deciding there is nothing to find is the caller's job, and none of these products will do it for you.

The judges agree on the order, not the numbers

Which judge you pick explains 2.2% of what varies; the provider explains 23.1%. Pairwise agreement is kappa 0.51 to 0.55 — moderate. Every absolute number here moves if you swap the judge. The ranking does not, except within the tie.

How it was run

204 questions: 96 base, 96 written to be harder, 12 with no answer at all as an honesty check. Frozen before the run. Every provider was asked for eight results, once each, caching off, on one day. Only the search provider varied — same prompts, same settings, same three judges throughout.

Judges were calibrated against human-settled pairs first, and none of them rated a keyword-stuffed page above zero. A failure that cannot be attributed to a specific step is recorded as unknown and charged to nobody, because every single-digit failure count this project has published turned out to be its own bug rather than a vendor's.
